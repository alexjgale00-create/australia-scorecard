GDP per capita, adjusted for purchasing power, is a rough measure of how much material comfort an average person here has. It's not a wellbeing measure on its own — it says nothing about how income is distributed, how many hours people work to earn it, or what it costs to live. When Australia's living-standards score slips against peers, it means the average Australian's slice of the economic pie is growing slower than a comparable slice elsewhere, even if the overall economy is still growing.
