Whether the majority population actually welcomes newcomers shapes daily life for a lot of Australians in a way that no institutional measure fully captures. This is the other half of this Scorecard's social cohesion cluster, paired with Cohesion — minority experience. It measures one thing precisely rather than a broad sentiment: the share of people who name immigrants or foreign workers as a group they would not want living next door — World Values Survey Wave 7, question Q21. Lower is better. The narrow framing is deliberate and the gauge is named for what it measures: an earlier version carried Gallup's broader "migrant acceptance" name, which turned out to assert an equivalence the underlying item does not support — Germany is the most accepting of the nine on this question and among the most negative on WVS's separate question about immigration's impact on the country. Each country reports its own fieldwork year, between 2017 and 2022, and the data is genuinely old; that WVS Wave 7 is the only source covering all nine peers at all is itself worth knowing.
