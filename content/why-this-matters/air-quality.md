Fine-particulate air pollution has a direct, well-established link to individual health outcomes — cardiovascular and respiratory disease, reduced life expectancy — and unlike most pollution measures, PM2.5 exposure is genuinely comparable across countries because it's population-weighted, not just a monitoring-station reading. It's also one of the more physically involuntary items in this dimension: unlike diet or exercise, a resident mostly can't opt out of the air where they live.
