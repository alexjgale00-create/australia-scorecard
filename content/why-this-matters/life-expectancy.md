Life expectancy is the single most integrative health statistic there is — it silently folds in the quality of a country's healthcare system, its rates of chronic disease, road and workplace safety, environmental exposure, and social conditions, all compressed into one number. It's also slow-moving and hard to fake: a country can't talk its way to a longer average life. When Australia's life expectancy score slips against peers, it's a lagging signal that something in the system — healthcare access, chronic disease management, injury prevention — is falling behind, even before any single cause is identifiable.
