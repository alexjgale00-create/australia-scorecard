Unlike assault or theft, a homicide's body count is hard to under-report. Cross-country comparisons still have to be read carefully, though: legal definitions and counting rules for homicide vary between countries, and UNODC's own research finds this affects comparability — the data isn't immune to the definitional problems that affect other crime categories, just less exposed to them. It's still the most cross-country-comparable safety statistic realistically available, even though it's a narrow slice of "personal safety" in the everyday sense. It's a floor, not a ceiling: a country can score well here and still have citizens who don't feel safe walking alone at night. Read alongside the rest of this dimension, not as a complete verdict on safety by itself.
